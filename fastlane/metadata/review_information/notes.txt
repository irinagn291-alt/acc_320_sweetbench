The app requests no permissions. There is no permission prompt at launch or later, and there is no usage-description string.

There is no login, no in-app purchase, no user-generated content shared with anyone else, no account deletion flow, and no App Tracking Transparency prompt. "Delete rotation" removes the local closet only. There is no account to delete.

Sweetbench is a dark, on-device log for shoes a person already owns. They add each shoe with a purchase price and an optional disposal value, pair two shoes, and log the distance of each outing. The bench shows a US-dollar cost per mile and, once both shoes have four logged runs and fourteen days, names the cheaper one. It is for someone keeping that record on their own iPhone or iPad.

No credentials or demo account are needed. On a new install the closet starts empty and the introduction is shown. Cold launch opens on a dimmed, non-tappable copy of the Mile screen. If loading takes a moment, a spinner appears over it. The first time, that gives way to a three-page introduction. Each page has a picture, a large capital headline, one sentence, "Skip", and a primary button. Page one is headline "PAIRS" and "Log miles on shoes you own. See what each pair costs per mile.", with primary button "Next". Page two is headline "MILE" and "Pair two shoes, then log the distance from today's run.", with primary button "Next". Page three is headline "SPLIT" and "After four runs and fourteen days on both shoes, the bench names the cheaper pair.", with primary button "Start". "Skip" on any page, or "Start" on the last page, marks the introduction done and opens Mile. "Next" only advances the page. There is no account step.

Mile is the home screen. The title is "MILE". With an empty closet the line is "Closet empty. Add a shoe you own." Two plates are titled "Left shoe" and "Right shoe", each with the grade "Need peers" and "No miles yet." There is no tab bar. Under "BENCH", open "Closet". The empty state is "The closet is empty." and "Add a shoe you own." Tap "Add shoe". The form has "Nickname", "Purchase price", and "Disposal value". "Save shoe" adds the shoe. A blank disposal value is saved as zero. Add a second shoe the same way. Each sheet has "Close" at the top left. Open "Pair shoes". The headline is "Pair" and the status is "Choose two shoes. Then log miles." Under "LEFT SHOE" and "RIGHT SHOE", tap "Choose" on two different shoes so each reads "Selected", then tap "Pair these". That files the pair and closes the sheet. After a pair exists, "Pair these" stays faded. "Pair again" can be tapped but does not replace the pair, and the status becomes "Pair refused. Bench already paired."

Back on Mile, each plate uses that shoe's nickname, a grade chip, and a US-dollar figure followed by "per mile". A paired shoe can show that figure before any distance is logged. The distance row uses the left shoe's nickname. The field prompt is "Type miles" and the unit reads "mi". Type a number above zero, tap "Log mile", then tap "Log" plus the right shoe's nickname. A successful log clears the field and adds an outing. "LATEST OUTING" shows the newest shoe name, distance, and "mi" or "km". "Undo outing" removes the newest outing and stays faded until at least one outing exists.

Open "Split call". The sentence is "Both shoes need four logged runs and fourteen days. Then the bench names the cheaper one." Each side is labelled "LEFT SHOE" or "RIGHT SHOE", with a count "of" "4" "runs" and an age "of" "14" "days". "Log a run" or "Log a run on" plus a nickname adds a distance of 1 on the shoe with fewer runs. A block labelled "Cheaper" shows the lower US-dollar cost only after both shoes show 4 of 4 runs and 14 of 14 days. A new calendar day updates ages. On Mile, once a split has been filed, "SPLIT CALL" shows the lower cost, the word "beats", the higher cost, and the cheaper shoe's nickname. Open "Ranks" for "Grades are relative to this closet." and "LOWEST COST" on the first shoe.
